# ADU Rules in Pennsylvania (research notes)

Compiled November 10, 2026. Research summary, not legal advice. Always confirm with the municipality's current zoning code. This file is the source for `shared/legal_status.csv` and the rule text in the tool.

## 1. Snapshot

| Level | Status (Nov 2026) | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| State | No statewide ADU law | Each municipality decides. HB 2186 and SB 1346 pending |
| State building code | 2021 I-Codes (PA UCC), effective Jan 1, 2026 | An ADU is a dwelling unit. Permits, inspections, certificate of occupancy (CO) |
| Philadelphia | Allowed, very narrow | Some single-family detached and semi-detached lots in Council Districts 1, 2, 3, 7, plus historic buildings. About 1 approval per year |
| Pittsburgh | Newly legal citywide | Bill 2025-1545 passed Oct 27, 2026, signed Oct 29, 2026 |
| Other municipalities | Patchwork | By right, special exception, family-only temporary units, or banned |

## 2. Who regulates ADUs

- The Municipalities Planning Code (MPC, Act 247 of 1968) covers zoning in every PA municipality except Philadelphia and Pittsburgh
- Philadelphia: own Zoning Code (Title 14). ADUs in 14-604(11)
- Pittsburgh: own Zoning Code (Title 9)
- So a bill that amends the MPC (SB 1346) would not reach Philadelphia or Pittsburgh. A bill that amends Title 53 (HB 2186) would

| Approval path | Who decides | For owners |
|---|---|---|
| By right | Zoning officer | Meet written standards, get a permit. No hearing |
| Special exception | Zoning Hearing Board | Hearing. Granted if criteria are met |
| Conditional use | Governing body | Hearing, more discretion |
| Use variance | Zoning Hearing Board | Must prove unnecessary hardship. Slow, costly, uncertain |
| Prohibited | None | Only a variance or a code change |

Many PA municipalities now require a variance for an ADU.

## 3. Common local standards

| Standard | Typical PA approach |
|---|---|
| Lot type | Single-family detached lot only (most common) |
| Number | 1 per lot. Pittsburgh now allows up to 2 |
| Configuration | Internal, attached addition, or detached. Some codes allow only conversion of existing structures |
| Size cap | Often 800 sq ft, and/or a share of the main house (e.g. 25%) |
| Owner occupancy | Owner lives in the main house or the ADU. Often a deed restriction |
| Occupant limits | Family-only, caregiver-only, or temporary (ECHO) units. Some require removal later |
| Min lot size | Philadelphia: 1,600 sq ft |
| Parking | 1 extra space is common. Increasingly waived |
| Design and entrance | One street-facing entrance, matching materials |
| Short-term rental | Often banned (under 30 days). HB 2186 still lets towns ban it |
| Utilities | Proof of sewer and water capacity. Big issue on septic |
| Conveyance | Cannot be sold apart from the main lot |

## 4. Building code (PA UCC)

- PA UCC (Act 45 of 1999), based on the 2021 International Codes, statewide since Jan 1, 2026
- One- and two-family dwellings follow the IRC
- Internal or attached ADU: the building becomes a two-family dwelling. 2021 IRC R302.3 needs a 1-hour fire separation
- Detached ADU: its own one-family dwelling. Walls near lot lines follow IRC Table R302.1
- Sprinklers not required in new one- and two-family homes (Act 1 of 2011)
- Permit, inspections, and a CO for any new dwelling
- Philadelphia L&I runs its own code, also based on the 2021 I-Codes
- Towns may adopt stricter amendments

## 5. Sewage and water (Act 537)

- New development needs sewage facilities planning (Sewage Facilities Planning Module Application Mailer). DEP decides the planning type or exemption
- Go through the municipality, not straight to DEP
- On-lot septic: the Sewage Enforcement Officer checks capacity. A new unit may need a bigger system
- Public sewer: tap or connection fees per equivalent dwelling unit
- Earlier state ADU bills (e.g. SB 1126) did not exempt ADUs from sewage rules

## 6. Philadelphia (Zoning Code 14-604(11))

By right, path A (all must be true):
- Base zoning RSA-5 or CMX-1
- Lot 1,600 sq ft or more, and the district otherwise allows 1 unit
- Single-family use in a detached or semi-detached building. Rowhouses excluded
- Council Districts 1, 2, 3, 7 only. The L&I checklist screens the /FNE, /NE, /FDO, /VDO, /NDO, /EDO overlays
- Inside the house, or in a detached accessory building (e.g. garage) that existed when the 2012 code took effect. No new backyard cottages

By right, path B: historic buildings (designated or contributing). Attached rowhouses eligible. May exceed 800 sq ft.

All Philadelphia ADUs:
- One per lot. 800 sq ft max (except historic)
- Owner lives in one of the units. Affidavit and deed restriction before final occupancy
- One street-facing entrance unless a second already existed
- Rental license and CO before renting
- Anything else needs a ZBA variance

Timeline:
- 2012: new code defines ADUs, but they are not legal anywhere
- 2019: Bill 190612 allows ADUs in historic buildings (enacted Oct 24, 2019)
- 2021: expansion bill passes, but about half of Council exempts their districts. Districts 1, 2, 3, 7 remain
- 2015-2025: about 1 ADU approved per year
- Jan 30, 2025: Bill 250042 introduced. Still in the Committee on Rules in 2026

Bill 250042 (pending): by right in existing single-family homes in Districts 1, 2, 3, 7 (others can opt in). No ZBA if the footprint barely changes. Owner occupancy stays. Size limits removed. Adds rowhouses.

## 7. Pittsburgh

- Before 2026: new ADUs not allowed. Garfield overlay pilot (2018-2020) gave 2 permits and no construction
- Bill 2025-1545: approved Oct 27, 2026, signed by Mayor O'Connor Oct 29, 2026
  - Inside a home, detached, or in an existing garage or basement
  - Citywide, by right for residences, community centers, religious facilities
  - Up to 2 ADUs per residential lot
  - No owner occupancy
  - Minimum parking removed citywide
  - Voluntary affordable housing bonus instead of mandatory inclusionary zoning
- Confirm size, height, and setbacks in the adopted text on Engage PGH

## 9. State bills (2025-26 session)

| | HB 2186 (Rep. Inglis) | SB 1346 (Sen. Rothman) |
|---|---|---|
| Amends | Title 53 | MPC |
| Covers Philadelphia and Pittsburgh | Yes | No (as written) |
| Core rule | 1 ADU per residential lot, by right | ADUs by right through the MPC |
| Status | Passed House 139-62, June 1, 2026. In the Senate | Introduced May 26, 2026. Passed Senate Urban Affairs and Housing 10-1 |

HB 2186, towns may:
- Set reasonable size, height, setback limits
- Ban short-term rentals under 30 days
- Keep safety and compatibility standards

HB 2186, towns may not:
- Require owner occupancy
- Require a family relationship
- Impose overly strict material rules
- Require an inside connection between units
- Require fixing existing nonconforming structures

Also: ADUs can be rented. They do not count toward density. HOA and deed rules still apply. RVs are not ADUs. Towns must adopt an ADU ordinance.

Outlook: no Senate movement since spring. The session ends Nov 30, 2026. Bills not passed die and must be reintroduced in 2027-28. Main fight: whether Philadelphia and Pittsburgh are included.

Related: HB 2109 (unrelated occupants, passed House), HB 2185 (2-4 units in single-family zones), HB 2155 (no parking minimums), SB 1281 (pre-approved plans, passed Senate), SB 1126 (2023-24, died).

## 10. Governor's Housing Action Plan (Feb 12, 2026)

- First statewide plan. Shortfall of about 185,000 homes by 2035. Need for about 450,000 new units
- Calls for updating the MPC, including ADU standards, and by-right ADUs, duplexes, triplexes in single-family zones
- Goals only. PSATS opposes state preemption

## 11. Checklist for a PA parcel

1. Find the municipality and zoning district
2. Read the ADU section: by right, special exception, conditional use, or not allowed
3. Check lot size, building type, owner occupancy, family rules, size cap
4. Check overlays (historic, floodplain, steep slope, Philadelphia district overlays)
5. Check HOA covenants and deed restrictions
6. Check sewer and water (Act 537 mailer, SEO if septic, tap fees)
7. Get UCC permits (fire separation, egress, CO)
8. Before renting: rental license, short-term rental rules
9. Track HB 2186
